Program overview

What the integration is, who it is for, and what changes for students
PLE REAP
Medicine

This instructional design sets out how PLE REAP Medicine adds a knowledge-enrichment layer to the Biochemistry course of the VMUF College of Medicine, starting with an orientation on October 14, 2026, 8:30 AM via Zoom. The department keeps its lectures, laboratory work and examinations. PLE REAP contributes two things: a study pack of flashcards, mnemonics and MCQs for each topic, released on the Saturday of the week before its post-test, and a Cheat Sheet after each rationale; and a synchronous session on the topic just taught, on Zoom or the QA App: an hour and a half of reinforcement by video or reading, then a 20-item post-test, with the rationale at 5:00 PM the same day.

Learner and needs analysis

Six facts about the learners, and how the design answers each
PLE REAP
Medicine

Six factors shape this design. Choose a factor to see what it means for the learners and how the design responds.

🎓
Factor 1 of 6
Stage of training
What it means for the learners
Students are early in the MD program. The licensure examination is years away, and Biochemistry is taught as a basic science.
↓
✔ Design response
Items are written in PLE style from the start, so students meet licensure-type questions on each topic while it is fresh, without adding new content to the course.
📅
Factor 2 of 6
A full course load
What it means for the learners
Biochemistry runs alongside other first-year courses, with lectures twice a week and frequent examinations.
↓
✔ Design response
One fixed two-hour slot per topic, on dates the department has already set aside, viewable on Zoom or the QA App, with a reminder the day before; everything else is short and self-paced on the phone.
🧠
Factor 3 of 6
Recall-heavy content
What it means for the learners
Pathways, enzymes, cofactors, inborn errors and normal values have to be remembered exactly.
↓
✔ Design response
Flashcards with spaced review, mnemonics for lists, pathways and classifications, released topic by topic in Saturday study packs, and a one-page Cheat Sheet of the must-know facts after the rationale.
🩺
Factor 4 of 6
Clinical correlation
What it means for the learners
The course pairs nearly every topic with a clinical or laboratory correlation.
↓
✔ Design response
Post-test and bank items include clinical vignettes and laboratory findings, the way the PLE asks them.
⏳
Factor 5 of 6
Exams close behind lectures
What it means for the learners
A shifting exam usually follows each topic within three to seven days.
↓
✔ Design response
The post-test sits between the lecture and the shifting exam, and the rationale arrives the same day, so the review days are used on real gaps.
🧩
Factor 6 of 6
Integration starts mid-course
What it means for the learners
The integration begins with the orientation on October 14, after Module I and most of Module II have been taught.
↓
✔ Design response
The KE 1 pack comes out on October 15, a Proteins Module pack on November 7, and KE 4 (Nov 11) is a cumulative post-test on the Proteins module. Module I is not part of the knowledge enhancement.

Study-pack MCQ · sample 1

A 45-year-old man taking methotrexate for psoriasis develops megaloblastic anemia. Which enzyme does the drug inhibit directly?

Methotrexate is a folate analogue that inhibits dihydrofolate reductase, depleting tetrahydrofolate and so impairing dTMP and purine synthesis. Thymidylate synthase is the target of 5-fluorouracil; ribonucleotide reductase is inhibited by hydroxyurea; HGPRT is a salvage enzyme that activates 6-mercaptopurine.
Study-pack MCQ · sample 2

A double-stranded DNA sample contains 30% adenine. What percentage of its bases is guanine?

By Chargaff’s rules, A = T, so A + T = 60%. That leaves 40% for G + C, and since G = C, guanine is 20%.
Study-pack MCQ · sample 3

Which change in a DNA sequence is a transversion?

A transversion swaps a purine for a pyrimidine or the reverse. Guanine (purine) to thymine (pyrimidine) is a transversion. The other three are transitions: purine to purine or pyrimidine to pyrimidine.

Frequently asked questions

GradesDo the post-test scores count toward my grade?

Yes. Post-test scores are expected to be added to your total Biochemistry grade. The Department of Biochemistry and the College of Medicine have the final say on how they are counted.

GradesDoes the Comprehensive Examination count too?

The department and the college will decide and announce it. Either way, it is your best check of all the KE topics before the PLE years.

Missed sessionsWhat if I miss a session?

If you have a reasonable excuse, you may still answer the post-test later the same day, up to 5:00 PM. Watch the recording in the QA App first.

Missed sessionsCan I answer the post-test after 5:00 PM?

No. The rationale is released at 5:00 PM, and the post-test closes at the same time, for everyone.

SessionsHow do I watch the video lecture?

Live on Zoom or in the QA App. Reading sessions are done in the QA App. The recording stays in the app afterwards.

SessionsHow will I know the link and the mode for each session?

A final announcement and reminder comes out the day before every session, with the link, the mode (video or reading) and the time.

SessionsWhy is KE 1 different?

Today is the orientation, at 8:30 AM via Zoom. The KE 1 study pack comes out on Thursday, October 15. The KE 1 video lecture and post-test are posted on Friday, October 16; the post-test is open for 24 hours, and the rationale comes out the next day, Saturday, October 17.

Study packsWhen do I get the flashcards, mnemonics and MCQs?

On the Saturday of the week before each post-test, one topic per pack. KE 1’s pack comes out on Thursday, October 15. There is no separate set released on the day before a session.

Study packsAre the post-test questions in the study pack?

No. Post-test items are new each time. The pack prepares you for the topic, not for specific questions.

Study packsDo I have to finish every earlier pack before each session?

No. Focus on the current topic’s pack; return to earlier packs on weekends and before the midterm, finals and April 17.

Taking the testCan I use my notes during the post-test?

No. The post-test is taken without references, like the licensure examination.

Taking the testWhat device do I need?

A phone or laptop with the QA App, charged, on a stable connection. Zoom is optional if you watch in the app.
